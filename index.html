Chapter 2 · My work

Teaching networks to learn physics.

Two years of gravitational lens modeling at Rutgers, from thesis to cluster-scale simulation.

Post-Baccalaureate Researcher

Rutgers Physics & Astronomy · Sep 2025 – Jun 2026

  • Scaled mock-lens generation from a laptop to the Amarel cluster with SLURM job arrays: 300,000 raw systems (100,000 each of SIS+shear, SIE and SIE+shear), curated to 79,398 shear-model systems with a magnification threshold.
  • Designed three Keras networks matched to lens multiplicity (4-, 7- and 13-feature inputs), trained with Adam, batch normalization and early stopping to a best validation loss of 0.0032 and MAE of 0.045 in 94–139 epochs.
  • Tested a network trained on one lens model against another and measured the drop to MAE 0.85–0.92, marking the limit of transfer across the isothermal family.
  • Built diagnostics (residual histograms, Q-Q plots, skew tests) and rotation and translation augmentation.

Undergraduate Student Researcher

Rutgers Physics & Astronomy · Sep 2024 – May 2025

  • Built a Python pipeline on the group’s gravlens library, drawing shear and ellipticity from Oguri & Marshall (2010) distributions for 9,000 simulated lenses across three isothermal models.
  • Mapped correlations across five lens parameters in corner plots, showing the learnable structure that motivated the neural network approach.

Contributed talk

Rutgers Physics & Astronomy · March 2025

  • Presented the full pipeline, from dataset generation to validation, to faculty, postdocs and students.

Chapter 3 · My Personal Projects

Case studies

Open a case study to see the question, the method and the result. The first two are my research; the others show the same habits on other problems.

Lens modeling at scale

Can a neural network recover lens parameters from simulated lensed systems? Keras, SLURM, gravlens.

+

The question

Can neural networks learn to recover lens parameters from simulated lensed systems, and how far can that be trusted?

The approach

Simulated lenses with the group’s gravlens library, scaled to the Amarel cluster with SLURM job arrays, then trained three Keras networks matched to lens multiplicity (4-, 7- and 13-feature inputs to 3, 6 and 12 outputs).

The result

Best validation loss 0.0032 and MAE 0.045 in 94–139 epochs, checked with residual histograms, Q-Q plots, skew tests, and rotation and translation augmentation.

Raw systems300,000
Curated (shear model)79,398

Where the network stops generalizing

A cross-model test that defined the transfer limit across isothermal lenses.

+

The question

How far does a network trained on one lens model carry over to another?

The approach

Trained on SIS+shear systems, then tested on SIE systems from the same isothermal family, using the same residual and Q-Q diagnostics.

The result

Error rose from an MAE of 0.045 to 0.85–0.92, which defined the transfer limit across the isothermal model family.

Same model (validation)0.045
Cross-model (SIE)0.85–0.92

Physics as a validation set

A projectile network checked against the closed-form solution.

+

The question

Does a neural network recover known physics, rather than just fit data?

The approach

A three-hidden-layer (64-neuron, ReLU) Keras regressor trained on 1,000,000 simulated trajectories to predict range from velocity and launch angle.

The result

Validated on 100,000 held-out trajectories against the kinematic solution: R² of 0.9999 and MAE of 0.86 m, converging in 10 epochs.

What makes a track popular

Exploratory analysis of 586,672 Spotify tracks in pandas.

+

The question

What separates the most popular tracks from the rest?

The approach

Cleaned a Kaggle dataset, engineered a year field, and compared 736 top-tier tracks (popularity above 80) with 585,936 others across six audio features using box plots, KDEs and a heatmap.

The result

Acousticness (r = −0.37) and loudness (r = 0.33) correlated most strongly with popularity, reported as correlation only.

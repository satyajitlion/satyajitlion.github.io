Machine learning for gravitational lens modeling

Training neural networks on 300,000 simulated lenses, toward time-delay cosmography. Keras, SLURM, pygravlens.

+
  • Scientific computing
  • Neural networks
  • HPC
  • Data engineering
  • Model evaluation

Problem

The time delays between the images of a strong gravitational lens can constrain the Hubble constant (H0), but fitting a detailed lens model to every system is computationally expensive. This project asks whether neural networks trained on simulated lenses can learn the mapping from observable quantities (image positions, time delays and redshifts) to the lens potential and deflection, as a faster route toward time-delay cosmography.

Status: mock-lens generation is complete and network training and evaluation are ongoing. Applying the trained models to real lens systems to constrain H0 is future work.

My role

Under Dr. Charles Keeton, I built the mock-lens generator on his pygravlens code, parallelized it with SLURM job arrays on Rutgers’ Amarel cluster, wrote the preprocessing, designed and trained the Keras networks, and built the diagnostics used to evaluate them.

Methodology

  • Simulated data. 300,000 lens systems: 100,000 each for three isothermal models (SIS + external shear, SIE, and SIE + shear), with lens redshift 0.2 to 0.5, source redshift 1 to 3, Einstein radius 1.0 to 1.5 arcsec and Planck18 distances. Ellipticity and shear are drawn from the distributions of Oguri & Marshall (2010). For each system an image position is drawn at random and mapped back to the source, all images are then solved for, and images with |μ| < 10-4 are discarded.
  • Preprocessing. Each dataset is split 80/20 into training and test sets. Systems are grouped by image count (1, 2 or 4), and inputs and targets are standardized using scalers fit on the training set only. The SIS+shear training set contains 79,398 systems.
  • Networks. One feed-forward network per image count, with batch normalization and ReLU activations, trained with Adam on a mean-squared-error loss. The learning rate drops on plateaus and training stops early (with a minimum number of epochs); 10% of the training set is held out for validation.
  • Evaluation. Residual histograms, Q-Q plots and skewness on the held-out test set. Matching SIE and SIE+shear datasets of the same size were also generated, so that a network trained on one isothermal model can be tested on another.
Three networks, one per image count (targets: lens potential and deflection at each image)

Impact

  • Validation. Best validation loss (MSE, standardized units): 0.003 for the double-image network (validation MAE 0.041; stopped at epoch 94, weights restored from epoch 79) and 0.010 for the quad-image network (validation MAE 0.070; stopped at epoch 139).
  • Held-out test set (SIS+shear). Residuals are close to unbiased with small scatter. Double-image: deflection-magnitude residual 0.007 ± 0.035 arcsec (mean ± standard deviation), potential residual 0.007 ± 0.061. Quad-image: -0.009 ± 0.060 arcsec and 0.024 ± 0.069.
  • Overfitting check. For double-image lenses, training and test scatter agree closely (potential 0.060 vs 0.061). For quads the test scatter is larger (0.049 vs 0.069), which is consistent with only 3,935 quad training systems.
  • Scope. These results are for networks trained and tested within the SIS+shear family. Testing across lens families and inferring H0 from real systems are the next steps.

Physics as a validation set

A projectile network checked against the closed-form solution.

+
  • Regression
  • Supervised learning
  • Mathematical validation

Problem

Does a neural network recover known physics, rather than just fit data?

My role

I built and trained the network on 1,000,000 simulated trajectories and validated it against the closed-form kinematic solution on a held-out set.

Methodology

A three-hidden-layer (64-neuron, ReLU) Keras regressor trained on 1,000,000 simulated trajectories to predict range from velocity and launch angle.

Impact

Validated on 100,000 held-out trajectories against the kinematic solution: R² of 0.9999 and MAE of 0.86 m, converging in 10 epochs.

Exploring audio characteristics associated with track popularity

Exploratory analysis of 586,672 Spotify tracks in pandas.

+
  • Data cleaning
  • EDA
  • Statistics
  • Data visualization

Problem

What separates the most popular tracks from the rest?

My role

I cleaned the 586,672-track Kaggle dataset in pandas, engineered a year field, and ran the comparison across six audio features.

Methodology

Cleaned a Kaggle dataset, engineered a year field, and compared 736 top-tier tracks (popularity above 80) with 585,936 others across six audio features using box plots, KDEs and a heatmap.

Impact

Acousticness (r = −0.37) and loudness (r = 0.33) correlated most strongly with popularity, reported as correlation only.
